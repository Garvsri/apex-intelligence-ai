
# Formula 1: Core Concepts

## Lap Times
A lap time measures how long a driver takes to complete one circuit.
Comparing lap times can help identify performance trends, but results
are affected by tyre condition, fuel load, traffic, weather, pit stops,
and safety-car periods. A raw average is not a complete measure of
driver performance.

## Tyre Compounds
Formula 1 dry-weather tyres have different compound characteristics.
Softer compounds generally prioritize grip and performance but tend
to wear faster. Harder compounds generally offer greater durability
but may have different warm-up and grip characteristics. Actual
performance depends on track conditions and tyre specifications.

## Pit Stops
A pit stop allows a team to change tyres and perform permitted
servicing. The total strategic cost includes time spent in the pit
lane, stationary service time, and the effect of rejoining traffic.
A pit stop's value depends on the expected pace advantage afterward.

## Undercut and Overcut
An undercut involves stopping before a rival to try to gain time on
fresh tyres while the rival remains on track. An overcut involves
staying out longer and attempting to gain time before stopping.
Neither strategy is guaranteed to work; traffic, tyre performance,
pit-lane time, and track position matter.

## Safety Car
A safety car can reduce gaps between competitors and change the
strategic cost of a pit stop. Lap times recorded during neutralized
running should not be interpreted as directly comparable to ordinary
green-flag lap times without accounting for the conditions.

## Comparing Drivers
A meaningful pace comparison should use suitable laps from comparable
conditions. Analysts should consider outliers, pit-in and pit-out laps,
traffic, tyre age, weather, and safety-car periods. Always report
the sample size and limitations of the analysis.
